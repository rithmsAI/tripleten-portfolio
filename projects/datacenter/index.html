The question behind
the numbers.

How should we interpret efficiency improvements when total demand keeps growing? I examine resource growth and location variation, then separate what the disclosures show from what they cannot establish.

01 / ESTABLISH BOUNDARIES

Start with the scope.

Corporate fiscal-year totals, owned datacenter clusters, and state electricity sales describe different populations. They remain separate throughout the analysis.

02 / KEEP MEASURES HONEST

Distinguish water metrics.

Withdrawal is water brought on site; consumption is the portion not discharged. Withdrawal per total kWh is a screening ratio, not WUE or a cooling-efficiency score.

03 / TEST THE INTERPRETATION

Show the tradeoffs.

Location differences and controlled PUE scenarios help explain why one ratio cannot settle a broader infrastructure sustainability question.

What if efficiency
improves—and demand grows?

Change the assumptions to see the arithmetic. PUE equals total facility energy divided by IT energy. Non-IT overhead includes power distribution and cooling; it is not cooling alone.

Judgment is part
of the technical work.

No predictive model is fitted here. Six corporate years and one location cross-section do not support a reliable cooling predictor. A future model needs measured facility-month data, IT load, weather, cooling type, and temporal and location holdouts.

Regional context

EIA's five-state retail-sales panel adds infrastructure context. All-sector calendar-year sales cannot be attributed to datacenters or combined with fiscal-year Microsoft totals into a market share.

Beyond operations

Hardware lifetime, reuse, embodied emissions, grid constraints and upstream water impacts also matter. This release does not turn incomplete evidence into a lifecycle score.

Reproducible by design

The repository includes source hashes, attributed numeric excerpts, a data dictionary, cleaning checks, an executed notebook and regenerated figures. Censored observations remain explicit.

Inspect the work.

The analysis is designed to be questioned and reproduced. Start with the notebook, then follow each calculation to its source.
